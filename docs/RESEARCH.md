# The research behind Intent

Every design decision in Intent traces back to a study, a well-documented practice, or an explicit judgement call. This page lists them, along with what each source does **not** show. Intent makes no medical claims: it can't "reset dopamine", treat ADHD or addiction, or make anyone smarter. What it can do is make the thing you meant to do easy, and the thing you didn't mean to do a little slower.

Evidence labels used in the app: **Strong** (large randomized trials or meta-analyses), **Good** (well-run experiments or careful field studies, often small), **Early** (pilot studies or single small experiments), **Mixed** (studies disagree), **Real-world example** (what a person says they do; not evidence that it works for everyone).

## Habits

| Finding | Source | What Intent does with it |
|---|---|---|
| 89% of 1,130 filmed phone interactions were started by the person, not a notification; people interacted about every 5 minutes, often without remembering it. | Heitmayer & Lahlou (2021), *Computers in Human Behavior* 116 | The intervention happens at the moment of reaching (the home screen, the pause), not only through notifications. |
| Young adults used phones ~85 times and ~5 hours a day; 55% of uses were under 30 s; estimates didn't match reality. | Andrews, Ellis, Shaw & Piwek (2015), *PLOS ONE* | Honest counts: unlocks, minutes in mindful apps, "Nth session today". |
| Self-control problems account for an estimated 31% of social media use; letting people set future limits reduced use (−22 min/day). | Allcott, Gentzkow & Song (2022), *American Economic Review* | Rules set while calm; loosening a rule waits (cool-off), tightening is instant. |
| A new daily habit took a median of 66 days (range 18–254) to feel automatic; missing a day didn't ruin it. | Lally et al. (2010), *European Journal of Social Psychology* | No streaks to lose; "Day N with Intent" with patience framing. |
| If-then plans had a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65, 94 tests). | Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* | "When I feel the pull, I'll…" plan, shown on the pause screen. |
| Putting feelings into words reduced amygdala response. | Lieberman et al. (2007), *Psychological Science* | "How are you feeling?" on the pause, with one kind suggestion per answer. Also Dr. Sahar Yousef's advice to intervene at the feeling, before the craving. |
| Dopamine is more about "wanting" than "liking". | Berridge & Robinson (2016), *American Psychologist* | A Mind lesson. Applying it to phones is an interpretation, and we say so. |

## What works

| Finding | Source | What Intent does with it |
|---|---|---|
| A short breathing delay before chosen apps → 57% fewer openings over 6 weeks (280 users); in a separate experiment, an easy *cancel* option mattered most and a reflective message alone did little. | Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen (2023), *PNAS*. Note: field data, early dropouts excluded, app maker co-authored. | The pause screen: one breath (6 s default), then a choice. "Not now" is the biggest button and is pinned at the bottom, always visible. |
| Blocking mobile internet for 2 weeks improved sustained attention, mental health and well-being (467 people, preregistered), but adherence was low. | Castelo et al. (2025), *PNAS Nexus* | Essentials stay instant; only chosen apps get friction. Strict tools exist (basic phone, blocking) but are opt-in. |
| A bundle of nudges reduced problematic use and screen time in a randomized trial. | Olson et al. (2022), *Int. J. of Mental Health and Addiction* | Several small nudges, each optional. |
| Grayscale cut screen time immediately (N = 112), more than time limits; no immediate change in well-being or grades. A second study: −20 min/day, more sense of control, less stress, unlocks unchanged. | Zimmermann & Sobolev (2023), *Cyberpsychology, Behavior, and Social Networking*; *Mobile Media & Communication* (2024) | Calm, nearly monochrome UI; guide to Android's own grayscale. Not marketed as a brain upgrade. |
| Limiting Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 min each per day for 3 weeks reduced loneliness and depression (143 students). | Hunt, Marx, Lipson & Young (2018), *J. of Social and Clinical Psychology* | Short default sessions (5 min); optional daily session limits. |

## Notifications and interruptions

| Finding | Source | What Intent does with it |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving a notification, even without interacting, disrupted an attention task. | Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert (2015), *J. of Experimental Psychology: HPP* | Quiet notifications checklist. Intent itself sends nothing except an optional "your minutes are up" reminder. |
| Alerts on + phone nearby for a week → more inattention and hyperactivity symptoms (221 people). | Kushlev, Proulx & Dunn (2016), *CHI* | Same, plus "park the phone" guidance for focus. |
| Three notification batches a day improved well-being; no notifications at all raised anxiety and FOMO (237 people). | Fitz et al. (2019), *Computers in Human Behavior* | Check-in times for email and work chat, not a total blackout; calls and people always get through. |
| Checking email 3×/day lowered daily stress (124 adults, within-person). | Kushlev & Dunn (2015), *Computers in Human Behavior* | Check-in times default to two 20-minute windows (12:30, 18:30). |
| Interruptions during tasks: up to 27% longer, twice the errors, twice the rise in anxiety; deferring to a task boundary helped. | Bailey & Konstan (2006), *Computers in Human Behavior* | Focus sessions; "check messages when you finish a piece of work". |
| Attention residue: switching before finishing leaves part of your attention behind. | Leroy (2009), *OBHDP* | One thing in view; focus sessions. |
| Average time on one screen before switching: ~2.5 min (2004) → ~47 s (2016 on); about half are self-interruptions. | Gloria Mark (2023), *Attention Span*; UC interview (2024) | Today's one thing on the home screen (attention follows goals). |

## Content and attention

| Finding | Source | What Intent does with it |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok-style short videos during a break impaired prospective memory (remembering planned actions); Twitter, YouTube and rest did not (N = 60). | Chiossi et al. (2023), *CHI* | Lesson; default to short sessions for short-video apps. |
| 2–4 minutes of negative news on Twitter/YouTube lowered mood and optimism; kindness content didn't. | Buchanan, Aknin, Lotun & Sandstrom (2021), *PLOS ONE* | "If you scroll, scroll on purpose" practice. |
| Heavy media multitaskers were worse at filtering distractors; later replications found smaller, inconsistent effects. | Ophir, Nass & Wagner (2009), *PNAS*; later meta-analyses | Lesson labeled **Mixed**. |
| "Brain drain": a phone's mere presence reduced cognitive capacity. Direct replications failed; one meta-analysis found a small cost, a larger one (33 studies, 4,368 people) found no overall effect. | Ward et al. (2017); Ruiz Pardo & Minda (2022); Böttger et al. (2023); Hartanto et al. (2024) | Labeled **Mixed**. We still recommend "out of reach", because within reach the phone gets used more (Heitmayer). |
| A phone on the table or in hand during a café conversation → less fulfilling conversation, less perceived empathy (100 pairs). | Misra, Cheng, Genevie & Yuan (2016), *Environment and Behavior* | Lesson. |
| Phones out during a meal with friends → more distraction, less enjoyment (300+ people, randomized). | Dwyer, Kushlev & Dunn (2018), *J. of Experimental Social Psychology* | Lesson. |

## Brain and recovery

| Finding | Source | What Intent does with it |
|---|---|---|
| Light-emitting e-reader before bed vs printed book: melatonin suppressed by ~55%, longer to fall asleep, later body clock, less alert next morning (12 adults). | Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy & Czeisler (2015), *PNAS* | Wind-down schedule. |
| No phone in the 30 min before bed improved sleep measures (pilot RCT, 38 people). | He et al. (2020), *PLOS ONE* | Wind-down; the 30/15 rule lesson. |
| Minds wandered 46.9% of the time; people were less happy while wandering (2,250 people). | Killingsworth & Gilbert (2010), *Science* | Lesson. |
| 67% of men and 25% of women chose a mild shock over 15 minutes alone with their thoughts. | Wilson et al. (2014), *Science* | Lesson; "wait in a queue without your phone". |
| A boring task before a creativity test → more creative ideas. | Mann & Cadman (2014), *Creativity Research Journal* | Lesson, labeled **Early**. |
| The default mode network supports imagination and planning and cooperates with control networks in creative thinking. | Beaty, Benedek, Silvia & Schacter (2016), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* | Lesson (applying it to phones is an interpretation). |
| A walk in nature improved directed attention more than an urban walk. | Berman, Jonides & Kaplan (2008), *Psychological Science* | Lesson; break mode. |
| A year of walking 3×/week grew the hippocampus ~2% in older adults (RCT, 120 people). | Erickson et al. (2011), *PNAS* | Lesson. |
| Sleep consolidates memory. | Diekelmann & Born (2010), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* | Lesson. |
| Psychological detachment from work predicts recovery and well-being. | Sonnentag & Fritz (2007), *J. of Occupational Health Psychology* | Break mode, the 3M lesson. |
| Trying to answer a question before learning the answer improves later recall, even when the guess is wrong (the pretesting effect). | Kornell, Hays & Bjork (2009), *J. of Experimental Psychology: LMC*; Richland, Kornell & Kao (2009), *J. of Experimental Psychology: Applied* | Every Mind lesson opens with a quick guess. |

## People (real-world examples, not evidence)

- **Warren Buffett** used a basic flip phone until 2020 and says he uses his iPhone mostly for calls (CNBC, 2020).
- **Bill Gates** gave his children phones at 14, kept phones off the dinner table, and is known for "Think Weeks" (The Mirror, 2017; WSJ, 2005).
- **Steve Jobs** limited technology at home (Nick Bilton, *The New York Times*, 2014).
- **Tim Cook** "gutted" his notifications after reading his Screen Time data (2018–2023 interviews).
- **Cal Newport** says he has never had a social media account; *Digital Minimalism* (2019).
- **Tristan Harris** / Center for Humane Technology: notifications only from people, tools-only home screen, launch other apps by typing, charge outside the bedroom.
- **Pavel Durov** has said he kept his phone on airplane mode or mute, no longer carries one day to day, and avoids screens in the morning (Lex Fridman podcast; Tucker Carlson interview, 2025).
- **Christopher Nolan** doesn't use email or own a smartphone; he says it gives him time to think (Hollywood Reporter, 2014; 2026 interviews).
- **Dr. Sahar Yousef** (cognitive neuroscientist, UC Berkeley): phones out of sight for performance, the 30/15 rule, intervene at the feeling, and 3M breaks (micro, meso, macro). Interview with Raj Shamani, *Figuring Out*.

## Android facts the design respects

- A launcher controls its own home screen. Apps opened from notifications, recents or links bypass it unless an accessibility service (Focus Guard) notices them. Intent says this plainly.
- Focus Guard listens only to `typeWindowStateChanged` with `canRetrieveWindowContent="false"`.
- Background activity starts are allowed for bound accessibility services, which is how the pause screens appear.
- Unlocking can resume the previous app instead of the home screen, so the unlock check-in is shown when home is actually visible, and Focus Guard re-checks the app in front after unlocking.
- Android 13+ restricts accessibility for sideloaded apps until the user allows "restricted settings".
- Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, hotspot and mobile data are device services. Intent never touches them; swipe down opens quick settings.

## What we decided not to build (yet)

- **Reels/Shorts filtering inside apps**: it needs reading screen content, breaks with every app update, and conflicts with "can't read your screen". The whole-app pause and short sessions are the honest alternative.
- **AI coaching or "productivity scores"** for your content: privacy cost, no demonstrated benefit.
- **Streaks, leaderboards, motivational notifications**: they create new reasons to check the phone.
- **A VPN or network filter**: one active VPN per device, fragile, and unnecessary for the core idea.
